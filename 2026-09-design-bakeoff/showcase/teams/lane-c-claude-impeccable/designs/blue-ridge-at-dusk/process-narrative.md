# Process narrative: Blue Ridge at Dusk

**Tools and setup.** We used Claude Code in the desktop app with the Impeccable design skill, which we installed ahead of time as a Claude Code plugin. We built the site as plain static HTML/CSS/JS so it runs locally with no build step. We started with `/impeccable init`, which scanned the repo and interviewed us, then wrote `PRODUCT.md`. That file records the audience (active builders and curious newcomers, weighted equally), the anti-goals (AI-startup gloss, AI-slop copy, corporate, insider-only), and the content rules. From there, Impeccable walked us through a series of ideation and feedback steps rather than jumping straight to code.

**Choosing a direction.** `/impeccable shape` generated seven concepts drawn from the group's own world (pegboard, wall calendar, name tag, topo map…). A dice roll picked one to lead (a workshop pegboard), and six outside "challenger" styles were dealt next to it. We found the literal concepts contrived, but liked the **WebGL Shader Portal** challenger for its particles, type and palette. On Impeccable's recommendation, we grounded it locally as **"Blue Ridge at dusk"**: a live particle field of the ridgelines west of Charlottesville, a haze-blue palette in place of the usual AI lilac, and a single ember accent kept for the next-meetup date, the Join buttons and a setting sun.

**Build and self-checking.** No image generation was available, so we built code-first: a custom WebGL field, a vectorized and recolored Studio IX logo, and a data-driven "TBA" state. The agent checked its own work with headless-Chrome screenshots at desktop and phone widths, a design-lint detector, and an independent **finish-reviewer** subagent that scored fixes over several rounds. Those rounds replaced button-like pills with hairline rows, restored the mission's welcome to newcomers, self-hosted the fonts in case venue Wi-Fi failed, softened mask edges, and kept the sun from ever sitting behind text.

**Room feedback.** The biggest improvements came from people looking at it. We moved About Us above the fold, and the mountains now thin behind the text instead of shrinking. We cut three nearby Meetup links down to one action per state and top-aligned the next-meetup block. We previewed a test meeting (Oct 27) with `?next=2026-10-27`, which led us to show the weekday instead of the year and to abbreviate the month only when the date would wrap.

**Output and verdict.** The result is a 189 KB self-contained `build/` folder ready to deploy anywhere, a `demo-snapshot/` with the decision page and screenshots, and a `DESIGN.md` written from the shipped code (0 detector findings). What the tools did well: the forced divergence broke us out of the generic look, and the adversarial reviewer caught real defects. Where they cost us: the review loops were slow, and the early concepts leaned literal. The human feedback in the room produced the clearest UX wins.
