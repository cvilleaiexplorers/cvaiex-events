About Us

We are a group of builders, learners, and enthusiasts who gather to:

  • Build practical AI applications and tools
  • Share technical knowledge and implementation experiences
  • Learn about and explore emerging AI technologies
  • Discover ways to enhance productivity and creativity
  • Support each other in creating AI-powered solutions

Our Mission

Our mission is to foster a community where people can develop AI building skills through hands-on projects, technical sharing, and collaborative learning, and discover practical ways to integrate AI tools into their work and daily lives. While our focus is on active building and implementation, we welcome anyone interested in learning these skills.

Activities and Directions

  • Exploring modern AI platforms and workflow tools
  • Technical demos and talks from AI builders
  • Technical book discussions and learning circles
  • Collaborative building sessions and group projects

Join Us

Whether you're actively building AI applications or eager to learn how, there's a place for you in our community. We emphasize practical skills and implementation while maintaining an inclusive environment for learning.

Get Involved

  • Attend our meetups
  • Share your AI experiences
  • Collaborate with other members
  • Present your projects or findings
  • Learn from peers

Trail Log

Every meetup so far, from the first gathering in March 2025. Unless noted, we met at Studio IX, 969 2nd St SE.

  1. #1Mar 12, 2025Practical AI in PracticeOwen Zanzal

    The kickoff meeting of Cville AI Explorers, a community for building AI skills through hands-on projects, technical sharing, and collaborative learning. Owen gave a short talk on applying AI in practice, followed by an open discussion to shape the group's direction.

    Event page

  2. #2Apr 22, 2025What is Prompt Engineering?John Berryman, Arcturus Labs

    John explained how user input gets translated into LLM logic, covering data sourcing, prompt assembly, and techniques from zero-shot and few-shot prompting to more advanced methods. The evening also introduced "breakout tracks", member-led side series such as a book club on Chip Huyen's AI Engineering, followed by an open forum.

    Event page

  3. #3May 13, 2025From Fuzz to Function: AI for Next-Gen AppsJustin Schroeder, FormKit Inc

    Justin presented AI as a way to build deterministic, reliable applications from messy human input, not just as a coding assistant. He covered AI routers, prompt chaining, multiplexing, and tool use as techniques for keeping AI systems controllable and predictable.

    Event page

  4. #4Jun 24, 2025Building Ladders: Extending Human Agency with AIDylan Isaac, Deque Systems

    Dylan described his move from accessibility coaching into AI engineering, and how he builds tools that automate drudgery so people can focus on meaningful work. His examples ranged from simple job-simplifying prompts to autonomous agents used in accessibility, development, and personal life.

    Event page

  5. #5Jul 22, 2025Demo DayEmily Smith, Michael Pfaffenberger, James Kovalenko

    Three community builders gave 20-minute demos. Emily showed Side Nerd, a text-message interface for volunteer tracking built on GPT models and AWS serverless. Michael showed Code Puppy, an AI coding agent that largely wrote itself, and James showed Perseus.tube, an AI-powered tool for searching and analyzing Attic Greek words.

    Event page

  6. #6Aug 26, 2025Transparent AI: Tools and Techniques for Taking the BS out of AI AdoptionDan Funk, SpiffWorks

    Dan showed how SpiffWorks combines BPMN flowcharts and Python into agentic workflows that business leaders and cross-functional teams can read and discuss. The session was a laptop-based lab that took apart how LLMs work, explained them without jargon, and gave attendees ways to teach AI concepts to others.

    Event page

  7. L&LSep 16, 2025Your README Has a Voice: Turning Repos Into Narrated Onboarding ToolsOwen Zanzal, Virtual Lunch & Learn #1

    Online, 12:00 PM

    A practice run of Owen's DevOps Days DC talk about RepoRad.io, an open-source CLI tool that reads a GitHub repo and turns it into narrated audio onboarding episodes using GPT-4 and OpenAI voice synthesis. He covered the architecture, keeping episodes current with GitHub Actions, and the tradeoffs of LLM narration in accuracy, privacy, and tone.

    Event page

  8. #7Sep 23, 2025What AI Teams Get Wrong about Vector SearchDoug Turnbull

    Doug addressed common misconceptions about vector search in RAG and recommendation systems, such as leaning too heavily on embeddings and ignoring established information-retrieval principles. He showed how to combine classical search techniques with modern AI to build vector-driven systems that are practical and reliable.

    Event page

  9. #8Oct 28, 2025From Reactive Tools to Proactive Teammates: The Next Shift in AIMichael J. (MJP) Prichard, Shift AI

    A fireside Q&A with one of Charlottesville's serial tech founders on AI moving from command-driven tools to proactive systems that anticipate human intent. Topics included building and funding AI startups in 2025, AI as an amplifier of human creativity, and lessons from scaling multiple companies.

    Event page

  10. #9Dec 9, 2025Tuning the Tuners: Building a Multi-Agent System for Search Relevance OptimizationEric Pugh, OpenSource Connections

    Eric walked through a multi-agent system he built with the Strands SDK using the "Agents as Tools" pattern, in which an orchestrator agent directs specialized agents to tune search relevance. He demoed the system live and shared lessons on model sensitivity, asynchronous user expectations, and making OpenSearch APIs agent-ready.

    Event page

  11. #10Feb 10, 2026Transformers ExplainedNish Tahir, Telus Digital

    Nish explained the transformer architecture behind ChatGPT, Claude, Ollama, and Stable Diffusion. His deep dive aimed to build intuition for how these models work without relying on math or jargon.

    Event page

  12. #11Mar 24, 2026Demo DayEugene Geis, Macon Pegram, Nish Tahir, Paul Otto, Jill Heinze

    Five community builders demoed AI projects. Eugene presented PraediVibe, a trust-focused AI analyst that writes clinically accurate SQL over VA healthcare data, and Macon presented a fully local, multimodal photo search engine. Nish showed a model trained to build Magic: The Gathering decks, Paul showed an LLM pipeline for analyzing Bluetooth and Wi-Fi protocol captures, and Jill showed a thinking companion for evaluating risk in AI use cases.

    Event page

  13. #12Apr 28, 2026Security in the Age of AIBirthday meetup with a panel of security, cloud, and AI practitioners

    The group's one-year anniversary meetup, with pizza sponsored by S&P Global, featured a panel on how AI introduces new security risks and amplifies old ones, including prompt injection, data poisoning, and supply-chain vulnerabilities. After a walkthrough of recent real-world incidents, the panel discussed where systems are breaking, what teams get wrong, and whether AI red teams are needed.

    Event page

  14. #13May 19, 2026Show Us Your SkillsMike Powers, plus community lightning talks

    Mike opened with a 30-minute overview of Agent Skills, an open standard for packaging procedural knowledge and workflows into portable folders. He covered the format, how to build a skill, and the surrounding plugin and marketplace ecosystem. Community members then gave roughly 10-minute lightning talks on skills they had built, adapted, or found useful.

    Event page

  15. #14Jun 30, 2026OpenClaw and Other Continuous AI AgentsJohn Athayde, Owen Zanzal, Stephen Levin

    This meeting focused on persistent AI agents that run continuously and need ongoing operational care. John covered six months of building on OpenClaw, including architecture, refactoring, and operational lessons. Owen walked through installing and evaluating Hermes Agent, and Stephen described using OpenClaw from his phone for social media content and quick code and website updates.

    Event page

  16. #15Jul 28, 2026Building a Second Brain / Domain-Specific AgentsKyle Carmitchel, Justin Schroeder

    Kyle presented Bosun, a personal agent built with the Claude Agent SDK. It manages his "second brain", connects to email, calendars, and finances, and takes part in family group chats over iMessage. Justin argued for replacing monolithic "Swiss Army knife" agents with small, composable, domain-specific agents that have narrow permissions and clean handoffs, for lower cost, better reliability, and safer deployment.

    Event page

  17. #16Aug 27, 2026Breaking Into Data Science & AI EngineeringPanel: Kerry Guerrero, Hunter Moore, Cory Everington, Patrick Harrison

    UVA School of Data Science, 1919 Ivy Rd

    A joint event with Charlottesville Data Science, in which experienced data scientists, AI engineers, and team leaders shared advice on landing a first or next job in the field. The monthly AI Explorers Book Club met beforehand, and the evening brought the two local communities together for networking.

    Event page

  18. #17Sep 29, 2026AI Design Bake-Off: Hands-On with AI Design ToolsHands-on team session led by volunteer team leads

    A hands-on session in which small teams each pick an AI-based front-end design tool and spend about 45 minutes using it to redesign the group's website, cvaiex.org, focusing on look, feel, and user experience. Teams then reconvene to demo their results and compare lessons learned. No coding or design background is required.

    Event page
